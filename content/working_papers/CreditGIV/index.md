abstract: "This paper studies the effects of idiosyncratic bank credit supply shocks on bank-level and aggregate credit dynamics and non-financial firms. We use comprehensive euro area loan-level data from AnaCredit to identify bank-level credit supply shocks and document persistent effects of these shocks on banks' lending portfolios and balance sheets. We aggregate the bank-level innovations to construct granular credit supply shocks that significantly affect credit dynamics at the country-sector and country level. We also find evidence of credit reallocation across banks in response to these granular shocks. Finally, we find that listed firms expand their capital stock following positive granular shocks, but do not respond significantly to negative shocks. Overall, our findings highlight how idiosyncratic credit supply shocks propagate through a concentrated banking system, shaping bank lending, aggregate credit dynamics, and firm investment."
