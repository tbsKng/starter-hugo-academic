abstract: "How much aggregate risk compensation can be traced to systemic bank runs? We answer this question using long-run estimates of retail- and wholesale-run probabilities and their real effects, measured independently of asset returns. We embed these moments in a rare-disaster model that jointly prices claims across the bank capital structure. Under the model-consistent mapping from contractions in lending and investment to aggregate disaster severity, run risk generates an annual equity premium of 91.46% of the historical equity-minus-bills premium. Using observed consumption declines directly instead yields 16.12% of the historical premium. This gap isolates the central empirical boundary: the aggregate asset-pricing importance of bank runs depends on how balance-sheet contractions map into consumption. The same state prices imply positive wholesale and competitive deposit spreads. The model premium declines with measured run risk after the introduction of federal deposit insurance, and run-risk valuations forecast real credit growth over one and five years but not consumption growth or excess returns. Systemic banking fragility can therefore be quantitatively important for aggregate asset prices while leaving distinct signatures across bank liabilities and real credit."
