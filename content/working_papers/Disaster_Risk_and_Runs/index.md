abstract: "How much of the equity premium can systemic bank runs explain? We treat runs as rare disasters whose probability and severity we measure directly, using two centuries of evidence on retail-deposit and wholesale-funding runs. Because bank equity is wiped out in a panic, its premium depends only on how often runs occur, how much damage they do, and how risk averse investors are. With moderate risk aversion, bank runs account for most of the historical equity premium in our benchmark. Retail runs contribute most of the premium, although wholesale runs have become the more frequent type since the 1990s. Bank runs therefore give rare-disaster models a disaster that can be observed rather than inferred from asset prices."
